# Getting Agency Teams to Actually Use AI

> The tools are ready. The people aren't. This is the hardest part of the role, and where most agency AI initiatives die.

## Overview

The Envato 2026 report makes it clear: AI adoption in creative agencies is wildly uneven. Web developers are at 65% daily use. Graphic designers are at 40%. And even among adopters, only 20% have any formal training. Most people are figuring it out alone, inconsistently, with no shared practices.

Your job isn't to force tools on people. It's to make AI feel like a natural extension of what they already do — then make the *non-AI way* feel like unnecessary friction.

## Adoption by Role — What You're Walking Into

### Developers (Easiest)
- Already at 65% daily AI use
- Familiar with CLI tools, code generation, automated testing
- Challenge: They've built *personal* workflows. Standardizing without taking away autonomy is key
- Approach: Show Claude Code as a power-up for what they already do, not a replacement for how they work

### Project Managers (Receptive)
- AI helps with the parts of their job they like least — status tracking, reporting, data wrangling
- 47% say AI frees time for strategy and creativity
- Challenge: Trust. PMs need to verify AI outputs before they'll stake client relationships on them
- Approach: Start with internal-only automations (status rollups, time tracking alerts) before client-facing ones

### Designers (Most Resistant)
- 40% daily use — lowest among creative roles
- 14% report active frustration with AI tools
- This is identity-level resistance: "If AI can do my job, what am I?"
- Challenge: AI threatens the *craft* narrative that senior designers built their careers on
- Approach: **Never position AI as replacing design skill.** Position it as:
  - Eliminating the boring parts (asset resizing, spec documentation, design token management)
  - Amplifying exploration (generate 20 variations in the time it takes to make 3)
  - Improving handoff (Figma MCP means their design intent actually reaches production)

### Creative Directors / Leadership
- Often the secret heavy users (58% of agency owners use AI daily)
- But may not want the team to know, or may fear client perception
- Challenge: Getting them to champion adoption publicly, not just benefit privately
- Approach: Frame it as competitive advantage. Agencies that don't adopt lose pitches to agencies that do

## The Adoption Playbook

### 1. Start with Pain, Not Tools
Don't lead with "we're implementing Claude Code." Lead with "what's the most annoying part of your week?" Then show how a tool eliminates that specific pain.

### 2. Build Champions, Not Mandates
Find 1-2 people per role who are curious. Give them early access. Let them become the team's proof that this works. Peer influence > top-down mandates.

### 3. Make AI Invisible Where Possible
The best AI adoption is when people don't realize they're "using AI." An n8n workflow that auto-generates their status report isn't "AI adoption" to them — it's just their report appearing on Monday morning.

### 4. Protect Creative Identity
Especially for designers: the narrative matters as much as the tool. "AI handles the mechanical work so you can focus on the creative decisions" is very different from "AI can now do design."

### 5. Measure and Share Wins
Track hours saved, error reduction, faster delivery. Share these publicly. Nothing drives adoption like "the team that uses this shipped 2 days faster."

### 6. Accept Uneven Adoption
Not everyone will adopt at the same pace. That's fine. The goal is to raise the floor (minimum viable tool use across the team), not force everyone to the ceiling.

## The Disclosure Problem

58% of agencies use AI in client work without telling clients. This is a ticking time bomb. As the AI integration lead, you should push for:
- A clear agency policy on AI disclosure
- Client-facing language that frames AI as a quality/speed advantage
- Positioning AI use as a feature of working with the agency, not a secret
