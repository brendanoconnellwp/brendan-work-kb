# First 30 Days at a Small Web Agency

> Week-by-week plan for showing up, absorbing, and building trust before building anything. Structured so you don't need to hold it in your head.

## The frame

You're joining a small, tight team mid-flight on multiple projects. Your first job isn't to add value — it's to *not slow them down* while you learn. Second job is to become the person who sees the whole picture, because nobody else has time to.

Don't pitch AI agents in week one. Listen. Map. Earn the rope.

## Week 1 — Land

**Goal:** Meet everyone, get oriented, don't break anything.

**Daily habits to establish:**
- Read `#general` and `#project-management` first thing each morning
- Join active project channels — at minimum, the two biggest active builds
- End-of-day daily dump into a dated note: what happened, what surprised you, what you don't understand yet
- Post in the team standup thread when it's active

**Conversations (30–45 min each, structured 1:1):**
- **Agency owner** — vision, what they hope you'll do, what success looks like in 90 days
- **PM / ops lead** — day-to-day ops, where they're drowning, what their ideal help looks like

**Read:**
- The operational snapshot (if one exists) — refresh it as you learn
- Every active project's Notion page (once access lands)
- Any agency playbook or onboarding doc that exists
- Last 2 weeks of messages in each active project channel

**Deliverable at end of week:** Write up what you learned. Correct or fill in any existing operational doc. If there's no operational doc, start one — even a single-page summary of who does what and what's currently active.

## Week 2 — Map

**Goal:** Understand how work flows, not just who does what.

**Conversations:**
- **Tech lead / lead developer** — stack reality, what breaks, where they're the bottleneck
- **Front-end dev / dev lead** — dev process, handoffs from design, what's painful
- **Design director** — design workflow, Figma → dev handoff, AI tool stance
- **Design lead** — where they sit between design and dev, their workflow

**Observe:**
- Sit in on at least 2–3 client calls across different project types
- Watch the standup thread — who posts what, what's left unsaid
- Watch a project sync end-to-end: from call to Notion update to Slack action item

**Map as you go:** For each project you're close enough to see, scaffold a project file with what you know. Leave sections empty if you don't know yet — don't fake it.

**Deliverable:** Rough project files for your 3–4 biggest active projects. Operational doc still fresh.

## Week 3 — Connect

**Goal:** Start seeing patterns across projects. Form an opinion.

**Conversations:**
- Any contractors or freelancers you've seen in channels — informal intros
- Any team members you haven't reached yet — institutional memory you don't want to miss

**Synthesize:**
- Look across your daily notes: what pain point has come up 3+ times?
- Cross-reference with [[Second Brain Gameplan]] agent candidates
- Form a hypothesis: *"The highest-leverage first build is X because Y"*

**Deliverable:** A one-page written hypothesis for the first agent or automation. Don't send it yet. Sit with it.

## Week 4 — Propose

**Goal:** Earn the right to build by walking in with sharp thinking.

**Conversations:**
- **Agency owner + PM/ops lead together** — share the hypothesis, get their reaction, revise
- Whoever the agent will serve (likely the ops/PM lead) — make sure it solves something real, not theoretical

**Ship (something small):**
- Not the agent yet. A proof of concept or a spec doc.
- Or: a visible small thing that uses your KB — e.g., a synthesized "what's moving across all projects" weekly digest you send to the owner. If they find it useful, that's the foot in the door.

**Deliverable:** First agent spec, signed off. First weekly digest sent.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- **Pitching AI in week 1.** You'll be "the AI guy" before you're a trusted teammate. Order matters.
- **Correcting people.** You're new. Things that look wrong often have history you don't know yet.
- **Trying to fix the KB for them.** The KB is *your* tool. Team-facing stuff comes much later.
- **Skipping the daily dump.** This is the cheapest, highest-compounding habit. Skip it and the whole system degrades.
- **Going heads-down.** This job requires being seen and trusted before it requires output. Be in channels. Comment. React. Show up.

## When something feels off

Write it down in your daily note. Don't act on it. Most "something feels off" in week 1–2 turns out to be you missing context. By week 4, if it still feels off, raise it — carefully.

## What to defer past 30 days

- Building any agent
- Proposing process changes
- Reorganizing the PM tool (Notion, Linear, whatever they use)
- Slack channel hygiene ideas
- Strong opinions on tooling

You'll have opinions on all of these by week 4. That's fine. The point isn't to suppress them — it's to be useful before being opinionated.
